Know your adversary

Threat Landscape

Threats come from many sources with different motives, skills and resources. Understanding who might target you, and why, helps you choose the right defenses.

Threat actors

Who poses a threat?

💰 Cybercriminals

Individuals or organized groups motivated mainly by financial gain. They run fraud, ransomware and data-theft operations, often at large scale and opportunistically.

Typical motive: Profit
Common methods: Phishing, ransomware, credential theft

🧑‍💼 Insider Threats

Current or former employees, contractors or partners who misuse legitimate access, either deliberately or accidentally. Accidental mistakes are far more common than malicious intent.

Typical motive: Grievance, gain, or simple error
Key defense: Least privilege and monitoring

🏛️ Nation-State Threats

Government-backed groups with substantial resources and patience. They pursue espionage, strategic advantage or disruption of critical infrastructure.

Typical motive: Espionage, geopolitical objectives
Characteristic: Advanced, persistent, well funded

📢 Hacktivists

Actors driven by political or social causes who aim to draw attention, embarrass organizations or disrupt services, for example through defacement or service-disruption campaigns.

Typical motive: Ideology or protest
Common effects: Outages, defacement, data leaks

🕸️ Botnets

Networks of compromised devices, from computers to smart-home gadgets, controlled remotely without owners' knowledge. They are rented or used for spam, fraud and large-scale traffic floods.

Typical use: DDoS, spam, credential attacks
Key defense: Patched, securely configured devices

🧭 Why it matters

Different actors call for different priorities. A small business is mostly exposed to criminals, while a defense contractor must also consider nation-state activity. Know your likely adversaries.

Takeaway: Match defenses to realistic threats

Risk management

How organizations assess cybersecurity risk

Risk is commonly expressed as the combination of how likely an event is and how severe its impact would be. Organizations follow a repeatable cycle:

  1. Identify assets – systems, data and services that matter.
  2. Identify threats and vulnerabilities – what could go wrong and where you are weak.
  3. Analyze likelihood and impact – estimate how probable and how damaging each risk is.
  4. Prioritize – rank risks, often with a risk matrix.
  5. Treat the risk – reduce, transfer (e.g. insurance), accept or avoid it.
  6. Monitor and review – repeat regularly as threats and systems change.
