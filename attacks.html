Cybersecurity Attacks

Understanding how attacks work in general terms helps you recognize them early. Each card below explains the concept, impact and warning signs. No attack instructions are provided, because the goal is defense.

Phishing Malware Ransomware DDoS SQL Injection MITM Brute Force Social Engineering Zero-Day

🎣 Phishing

Human-targetedEmail / SMS / Voice
Description
Deceptive messages that pretend to come from a trusted organization or person in order to trick recipients into revealing information or taking a harmful action.
How it generally works
The message creates urgency or fear, such as a "locked account" or "unpaid invoice", and directs the recipient to a look-alike page, an attachment or a reply. Targeted versions (spear phishing) use personal details to seem more convincing.
Potential impact
Stolen account credentials, financial fraud, malware infections and a foothold for larger breaches.
Warning signs
Unexpected requests, pressure to act immediately, mismatched sender addresses or link destinations, generic greetings, spelling errors and unusual attachments.

🦠 Malware

Software-basedViruses / Trojans / Spyware
Description
Malicious software designed to damage systems, spy on activity, or give unauthorized parties access to a device or network.
How it generally works
Malware typically arrives through infected downloads, email attachments, compromised websites or removable media, then runs quietly in the background, often trying to persist after a restart.
Potential impact
Data theft, loss of privacy, degraded performance, system damage and use of the device in larger criminal operations.
Warning signs
Sluggish performance, unexpected pop-ups, unknown programs, disabled security tools, unusual network activity and browser settings that change on their own.

🔒 Ransomware

ExtortionData encryption
Description
A type of malware that locks files or systems, and sometimes steals data, then demands payment to restore access or prevent publication.
How it generally works
After gaining access through phishing, exposed remote services or unpatched software, attackers spread within a network and then trigger the lock-out across as many systems as possible.
Potential impact
Operational shutdown, permanent data loss without backups, regulatory penalties, recovery costs and reputational harm.
Warning signs
Files that cannot be opened or have unfamiliar extensions, ransom notes, sudden mass file changes, and security tools or backups being disabled.

🌊 DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service)

AvailabilityNetwork / Application layer
Description
An attempt to make a website, application or network unavailable by overwhelming it with traffic from many sources at once.
How it generally works
A large number of compromised or coordinated devices send more requests than the target can handle, so legitimate users cannot get through.
Potential impact
Service downtime, lost revenue, customer frustration and diverted attention that may mask other attacks.
Warning signs
Sudden traffic spikes from unusual locations, extremely slow or unreachable services, and repeated identical requests in logs.

🗄️ SQL Injection

Application flawDatabases
Description
A weakness in web applications where untrusted user input is handled unsafely by a database, allowing it to be interpreted as part of a database command.
How it generally works
The risk appears when an application mixes user-supplied text directly with database queries without proper validation. Secure coding practices remove this class of flaw entirely.
Potential impact
Exposure or alteration of stored data, such as customer records, and potential loss of trust and legal consequences.
Warning signs
Unusual database errors, unexpected query patterns in logs, spikes in failed requests to input forms, and unexplained data changes.

🕵️ Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)

InterceptionNetwork
Description
An attack where a third party secretly positions itself between two communicating parties to observe or alter the exchange.
How it generally works
It is most feasible on unprotected or untrusted networks, such as open public Wi-Fi, where traffic is not properly encrypted or identities are not verified.
Potential impact
Exposure of private messages, session hijacking and tampering with transactions.
Warning signs
Browser certificate warnings, unexpected disconnections, unfamiliar networks with names similar to trusted ones, and pages not using HTTPS.

🔑 Brute Force

Credential-basedGuessing
Description
Repeated, automated guessing of passwords or keys until a correct one is found.
How it generally works
Success depends on weak, short or reused passwords and on systems that allow unlimited login attempts. Strong unique passwords and lockout controls make it impractical.
Potential impact
Unauthorized account access, data exposure and lateral movement to other services.
Warning signs
Large numbers of failed logins, attempts from many IP addresses, account lockouts and logins at unusual times.

🎭 Social Engineering

Human-targetedManipulation
Description
Psychological manipulation that persuades people to break normal security procedures or share confidential information.
How it generally works
Attackers pose as colleagues, support staff or authorities, using trust, authority, curiosity or urgency. It can occur by phone, in person, online or through messaging apps.
Potential impact
Unauthorized access, fraudulent payments, disclosure of confidential data and compromised accounts.
Warning signs
Unsolicited requests for passwords or codes, pressure to bypass procedure, claims of authority that cannot be verified, and too-good-to-be-true offers.

⏳ Zero-Day Attacks

Unknown flawSoftware vulnerabilities
Description
Attacks that take advantage of a software vulnerability unknown to the vendor, so no official fix exists when the attack begins.
How it generally works
Because the flaw is undisclosed, standard signature-based defenses may not recognize it. Once discovered, vendors race to release patches and defenders to apply them.
Potential impact
Compromise of fully updated systems, targeted espionage or disruption before defenses are available.
Warning signs
Unexpected crashes, abnormal system behavior, unusual outbound connections, and alerts from behavior-based detection tools.
