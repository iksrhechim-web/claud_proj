When something goes wrong

Incident Response

If you suspect a cyberattack, a calm and structured response limits damage and speeds recovery. Follow these six phases, adapted from widely used incident handling frameworks.

The process

Six steps of incident response

  1. 1
    Phase 01

    Detect

    Notice that something is wrong through alerts, monitoring, or a user report.

    • Review security alerts and logs
    • Encourage fast, blame-free reporting
    • Record the time and what was observed
  2. 2
    Phase 02

    Identify

    Confirm whether it is a real incident and understand its scope and severity.

    • Determine affected systems, accounts and data
    • Classify severity and assign an owner
    • Preserve evidence and keep notes
  3. 3
    Phase 03

    Contain

    Stop the problem from spreading while preserving evidence.

    • Isolate affected devices from the network
    • Disable compromised accounts
    • Do not wipe systems before evidence is captured
  4. 4
    Phase 04

    Eradicate

    Remove the cause of the incident and close the way in.

    • Remove malicious software or unauthorized access
    • Patch the exploited weakness
    • Reset credentials that may be exposed
  5. 5
    Phase 05

    Recover

    Safely restore normal operations and confirm systems are clean.

    • Restore from verified, clean backups
    • Bring systems back in stages
    • Monitor closely for signs of repeat activity
  6. 6
    Phase 06

    Review

    Learn from the incident so it is less likely, or less damaging, next time.

    • Hold a lessons-learned meeting
    • Update plans, controls and training
    • Meet any legal or reporting obligations

Be prepared

Why organizations need an incident response plan

Incidents are stressful and time-critical. Deciding roles and steps in advance means people can act quickly instead of improvising.

⏱️

Faster response

Clear steps reduce confusion and shorten the time an attacker stays active.

📉

Less damage

Quick containment limits financial loss, downtime and data exposure.

⚖️

Compliance

Many laws and regulations require timely breach notification and documented procedures.

🤝

Trust

A confident, transparent response protects customer and public confidence.

A good plan names responsible people, contact lists, communication templates and escalation paths, and it is practiced through regular exercises.
